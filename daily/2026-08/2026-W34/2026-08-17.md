## 今日知识点预习 (15-20 min · 详读)

四段式：是什么 / 为什么今天用 / 怎么用 / 坑。

### 1. @dataclass：自动生成样板代码

- **是什么**：在类上写 `@dataclass`，Python 自动帮你生成 `__init__`、`__repr__`、`__eq__`。你只写字段声明，不用手写构造函数。
- **为什么今天用**：你 Day 6 写过 `Student` 类——想想当时手写的 `__init__`（参数、赋值、判空、`__str__`）有多少样板代码。dataclass 一句话全给你生成。而且第 4 周你要用它对接 LLM 结构化输出，今天不学会，到那时是双倍痛苦。
- **怎么用**：
  ```python
  from dataclasses import dataclass

  @dataclass
  class Student:
      name: str
      age: int

  s = Student("张三", 18)          # __init__ 自动生成，不用你写
  print(s)                         # Student(name='张三', age=18)  __repr__ 自动生成
  print(s == Student("张三", 18))  # True  __eq__ 自动生成（字段逐个比较）
  ```
- **坑**：`Student()` 不传参数会报 `TypeError: missing 1 required positional argument: 'name'`——没默认值的字段必须传。别以为 dataclass 会把字段变可选。

### 2. 字段默认值：`default=...` 与 `field(default_factory=...)`

- **是什么**：字段声明里可以直接给默认值 `name: str = "无名"`。但**可变默认值（list/dict/set）不能直接写**，必须 `scores: list = field(default_factory=list)`。
- **为什么今天用**：Day 4 你学过「可变默认参数」的坑——`def f(x=[])` 所有调用共享同一个列表。dataclass 里 `scores: list = []` 是同一个坑换个马甲：所有不传 scores 的实例共享同一个列表，给 A 加分数 B 也能看到。`default_factory` 每次实例化时调 `list()` 生成一个全新的列表。
- **怎么用**：
  ```python
  from dataclasses import dataclass, field

  @dataclass
  class Student:
      name: str
      scores: list = field(default_factory=list)   # 每次实例化新列表

  a = Student("张三"); a.scores.append(90)
  b = Student("李四")
  print(b.scores)   # [] —— 李四的列表是全新的，看不到张三的 90
  ```
- **坑**：写成 `scores: list = []` 不报错但全是坑（共享列表）；写成 `scores = field(default_factory=list)` 忘掉类型注解也行但丢失类型信息；`field(default=[])` 是错的——default 只放不可变值。

### 3. frozen=True：只读对象

- **是什么**：`@dataclass(frozen=True)` 让实例不可变——创建后改属性抛 `FrozenInstanceError`。
- **为什么今天用**：程序里有些对象创建完就不该改（比如坐标、配置项、API 响应）。frozen 让「不该改」变成「改不了」——编译器/运行时帮你拦住手滑。面试问「怎么让 Python 对象只读」答 frozen=True。
- **怎么用**：
  ```python
  from dataclasses import dataclass

  @dataclass(frozen=True)
  class Point:
      x: float
      y: float

  p = Point(1.0, 2.0)
  p.x = 5.0   # dataclasses.FrozenInstanceError: cannot assign to field 'x'
  ```
- **坑**：frozen 类里的 list 字段照样能改（`p.items.append(...)` 不报错）——frozen 只管字段绑定，不管内容可变性。想要深冻结要自己写，今天不用管。

### 4. asdict / 与 dict 互转：dataclass ↔ JSON

- **是什么**：`dataclasses.asdict(s)` 把实例转成普通 dict；反过来从 dict 构造实例就是 `Student(**d)` 或手动取值。
- **为什么今天用**：API 收发、JSON 存盘、数据库读写——外面全是 dict/JSON，里面是 dataclass。第 3 周 FastAPI 的响应模型、第 4 周把 LLM 的 JSON 装进 dataclass，全靠这两个方向。
- **怎么用**：
  ```python
  from dataclasses import asdict

  s = Student("张三", [90, 85])
  d = asdict(s)          # {'name': '张三', 'scores': [90, 85]}
  print(d["name"])       # 当 dict 用
  s2 = Student(**d)      # 关键字展开，转回 Student
  print(s2 == s)         # True
  ```
- **坑**：`asdict` 是**浅拷贝**（嵌套的 list 是新对象，但嵌套的 dataclass 会递归转）；`Student(**d)` 要求 dict 的 key 和字段名完全一致，多一个 key 报 `TypeError: unexpected keyword argument`。

**Measure Twice, Cut Once**

Leo was sixteen when his uncle Marco took him into the workshop. Marco was a cabinet maker — he built tables, chairs, and bookshelves that lasted for fifty years. His nickname in the town was "The Measurer."

Leo did not like measuring. It felt slow. A ruler in one hand, a pencil in the other, checking and rechecking — it looked like wasting time. So Leo cut first and measured later. If the wood was a little short, he said, "Close enough. No one will see it."

His first bookshelf came out crooked. The left side was two centimeters shorter than the right. His uncle looked at it and said nothing. He just took out his pencil, drew a straight line, and wrote two numbers: 120.5 and 120.5.

"Two centimeters," Marco said. "The shelf will rock on the floor forever."

The next week, Leo built a small table. He was careful this time — for half an hour. Then he got bored and started cutting fast again. When he put the four legs together, one leg was shorter than the others. The table stood on three legs and wobbled.

Leo threw his ruler on the bench. "This is impossible! I measure, and it still goes wrong!"

Marco picked up the ruler and gave it back. "You measured once. Once is not measuring. Once is guessing."

"Then how many times?"

"Twice. The first time, you measure the wood — to learn its true length. The second time, you measure yourself — to check that your eyes did not lie to you. A man who measures once is measuring what he hopes. A man who measures twice is measuring what is real."

"Measuring myself?" Leo asked. "What does that mean?"

"It means you check your own work. You read the number again. You look at the pencil mark before you cut. You ask: 'Did I read this right, or did I just want it to be right?'"

Leo tried it on the next shelf. He measured the wood: 80.3. He measured again: 80.3. He marked the line, then looked at the mark once more before cutting. The cut was perfect. The shelf fit like it was born there.

Years later, Leo ran his own workshop. His students asked him why he was so slow with the ruler. "Because wood has no memory," he said. "Once you cut, it cannot go back. Measure twice, cut once — the second measurement is the one that saves you."

And when a student cut a board too short and wanted to hide it, Leo smiled and said: "Good. That board just taught you what one measurement costs. Now you will never do it again."
